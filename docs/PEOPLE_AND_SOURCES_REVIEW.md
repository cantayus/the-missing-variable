# People, quotations and sources: for review

The brief asked that only well-regarded people without scandal be featured, and that everything said about them be true. This file is here so that someone other than the author of the text can check both.

## What was and was not checked

- Each recording names real people and one piece of their work. The names, dates and places were written from general knowledge. A sample was looked up during the build. **Most were not individually confirmed against a primary source.**
- Nothing is quoted from anyone in a recording. The recordings describe the work in the third person.
- The title-screen quotations (22) are short, attributed, and were kept only where the wording and source were known. They are listed below.
- The list of sources in the game gives one citation per session, as text.

## Names taken out

Passing mentions of these people were removed or reworded so that no name appears: Marvin Minsky, John Searle, Francis Galton, Karl Pearson. The ideas are still described (the 1969 book on perceptrons, the Chinese room, regression to the mean, the histogram).

## Names worth a second look

None of these is known to the author of this file to have a problem, but each is either a living public figure or a person whose reputation has been argued over, so a decision should be made by a person:

- Lawrence Page and Sergey Brin (session II-10, PageRank). Founders of a very large company.
- Researchers named with their employers in Parts III and IV (OpenAI, Google, DeepMind, Meta, Microsoft, Anthropic). The recordings describe published papers only.
- William Playfair (session I-3 and one Field Guide entry). The inventor of the line chart and bar chart, and by most accounts a rogue in his business dealings. He died in 1823.
- Jürgen Schmidhuber (session III-10, with Sepp Hochreiter). Known for disputes over credit in the field, not for misconduct.
- Garry Kasparov and Lee Sedol are mentioned in two finds as the human players in famous matches.
